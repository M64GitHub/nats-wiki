## Comment — @Jarema (2022-10-18) [marked as answer]

Hey!

`cluster` routes are meant to be used only for other nodes (servers) to connect and form a cluster.

Clients should connect to the listen URL of one of the nodes. In your example it's `192.168.0.3:4222`.

Docs are here: https://docs.nats.io/running-a-nats-service/configuration/clustering

Keep in mind that your cluster should have at least 3 nodes.
If you want master/slave hub/spoke architecture, [leafnodes](https://docs.nats.io/running-a-nats-service/configuration/leafnodes) might be a better solution.

### Reply — @Jarema (2022-10-18)

When connecting to any of the nodes, Client will get information on other nodes in the cluster. That's why your `subscribe` example works by automatically reconnecting.

If you need to be able to **connect** to the cluster, no matter which servers are up, using only one URL, that's out of the scope of servers setup. 
You probably need something like a DNS with multiple entries, or [something relevant to your setup](https://docs.nats.io/running-a-nats-service/nats-kubernetes/nats-external-nlb#using-a-load-balancer-for-external-access-to-nats). 

You can also pass more URLs to the connect `nats pub foo data  --server localhost:4222,localhost:4223` and client will connect to the one that is available.
